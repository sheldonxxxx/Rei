Why We Moved Our Build Cache to Object Storage

Posted on 12 September 2026 by Priya Raman

For three years our continuous integration runners kept their build cache on a shared network drive. It worked until it did not: the drive filled every few weeks, and a full drive meant every pipeline fell back to a cold build that took forty minutes instead of six.

What we tried first

We added a nightly cleanup job that deleted cache entries older than seven days. That bought us a month. Then the monorepo grew another two services and the drive was full again by Thursday each week.

We also tried giving each runner its own local cache. Hit rates collapsed to about 30 percent because jobs rarely landed on the same runner twice.

The move

The cache now lives in an object storage bucket with a lifecycle rule that expires objects after 14 days. Runners read through a small local proxy that keeps the hottest 20 GB on disk.

The median pipeline went from 11 minutes to 7 minutes after the change, and we have not had a cache-full incident since.

What it cost

Storage and request charges come to roughly 38 dollars a month for our volume, which is less than the network drive's support contract alone.

The proxy matters more than the bucket. Without it, every cache read is a round trip to the region.

Would we do it again?

Yes, but we would set up the local proxy on day one instead of week three.
